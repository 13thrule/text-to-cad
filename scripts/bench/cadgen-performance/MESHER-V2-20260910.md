# Mesher v2 quality and native comparison

Keep the JavaScript mesher as the canonical implementation. The measured
carrier, gear, seam and singularity defects are fixed. Native OCCT is faster
on the nine-part fixture, but its complete Python delivery pipeline is slower
on the curved corpus at default detail and uses more process memory. These
results support the narrow fixes; they do not establish general FreeCAD visual
parity or a continuous surface-error guarantee.

## Scope and provenance

Final raw reports are [nine-part](results/mesher-v2-final-nine-20260910.json.gz)
and [curved](results/mesher-v2-final-curved-20260910.json.gz), with six sibling
JS, OCCT and decoder reports for each. They were recorded in a coordinated
window without competing builds or browser loads. Both report unchanged
runtime sources and byte determinism across all repetitions and two fresh
processes. Both use source fingerprint
`4f7c9a91a53ee495944dafe2fb70ebb41e37b3abfa7c9165605a328f27fb0b86`,
Git HEAD `64edaabc09e3ef9fcf97632dec14bbddb42c20c2` plus the recorded source
changes, Node v26.7.0, Python 3.13.13, build123d 0.11.1 and
cadquery-ocp 7.9.3.1.1 on arm64 macOS 26.5.1. The mesher identity is 2; the
transport remains TESS v3 and the release version is unchanged.

The nine-part BREP/SURF inputs are the same immutable snapshot used in the
[original v1 study](RESULTS-20260910.md#native-occt-experiment). The separate
seven-shape corpus contains a sphere with poles, a cone with an apex, a torus
with periodic seams, a perforated plate, a B-spline loft, that loft with a
cylindrical trim, and a spline revolution with a seam. `mesh_corpus.py`
reproduces these small private inputs under `models/`, without model/store
publication. Intermediate diagnostics used a different fingerprint and are
not part of the retained final report families.

## Correctness changes

Planar faces are triangulated again from their final shared trim points.
Previously, adding exact arc points to an old triangle fan could cross its
diagonal and create overlapping or inverted triangles around holes. Boundary
references still use the shared edge fractions and stable face/edge ordinals.

Source-precision snapping aligns curved pcurve bounds and grid intersections
before refinement, preventing slivers that collapse in Float32 transport.
Sphere/cone patches ending at a pole use a regular grid and a collapsed pole
row; analytic primitive normals retain their limiting direction there. Short
open edges and distinct corners are preserved independently of display chord
tolerance. These deterministic algorithm changes advance the mesher cache
identity from 1 to 2.

| Nine-part audit | v1 coarse | v2 coarse | v1 default | v2 default |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Degenerate triangles | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Unmatched quantized edges | 6 | 0 | 6 | 0 |
| Inconsistent welded-edge winding | 3 | 0 | 3 | 0 |
| Triangle/normal orientation flags | 60 | 0 | 32 | 0 |
| Triangles | 6,188 | 6,398 | 6,452 | 6,482 |

All seven structure checks are zero for **both algorithms, both corpora and
both LODs** in the final reports: the four checks above, non-unit normals,
invalid face references and invalid side references. The native extraction
discards three Float32-collapsed pole/apex triangles per curved-corpus LOD;
that filtering is included in its timed extraction and recorded explicitly.
The JS result requires no output triangle filtering.

The edge audit welds on `max(1e-7 mm, component.scale * 1e-6)`, so it remains
a diagnostic rather than a topological proof. Exact OCCT volume and face-area
queries provide separate checks. At default detail, maximum component volume
error is 0.986% JS / 0.516% native on the nine-part fixture, and 0.617% /
0.604% on the curved corpus. Maximum per-face area error is 0.449% / 0.516%
and 0.272% / 0.275%, respectively.

## Common sampled surface checks

Equal chord inputs alone are insufficient: on both B-spline shapes, a native
input chord of 0.028142 mm produces 0.037466 mm sampled deviation, 1.33 times
that input. Those values appear in the final coarse native rows. The final
native runs explicitly use
`--native-chord-scale 0.5`, relative to the JS physical chord
`component.scale * chordTolerance`; both use angle 0.35 radians and native
parallel meshing is disabled. This gives a shared sampled-error criterion,
not equal triangle distributions or equal visual quality. Native is often
finer than JS at these settings.

Outside timing, every used vertex, every triangle-edge midpoint and every
triangle centroid is projected onto its exact OCCT face surface. Singular
corners and exact bounded-face distance fallbacks handle undefined or distant
surface extrema. This checks the sampled underlying surface; area, winding
and edge checks assess trimming separately.

| Default-detail shape | Reference chord mm | JS sample maximum mm | Native sample maximum mm |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Perforated plate | 0.191701 | 0.131914 | 0.047306 |
| B-spline loft | 0.028142 | 0.022877 | 0.021981 |
| Trimmed B-spline | 0.028142 | 0.022877 | 0.021981 |
| Cone apex | 0.030000 | 0.008018 | 0.010409 |
| Sphere poles | 0.033541 | 0.022311 | 0.012610 |
| Torus seams | 0.036000 | 0.026004 | 0.016372 |
| Spline revolution | 0.028391 | 0.024082 | 0.012455 |

Both algorithms satisfy the requested reference chord at every sampled point
for the curved corpus at both LODs and the nine-part fixture at default LOD.
The coarse JS ring has a 0.569330 mm sample against a 0.565526 mm request:
**1.0067 times the requested chord**. That small exceedance remains visible in
the raw report. No result here proves a continuous facet/Hausdorff bound,
screen appearance, transformed picking, or coverage of arbitrary imported STEP.

## Complete CPU delivery and size

Each number is the median of six sums over unique components: three samples
in each of two fresh process trials. The pipeline includes file reads,
SURF parsing or private BREP reconstruction, meshing, native Python extraction
where needed, and encoding. It excludes process startup, quality audits,
temporary-file writes, network, browser messaging, main-thread work and GPU
uploads. Filesystem reads are normally page-cache hits. The samples are
aggregated component timings, not a single observed assembly load.

| Corpus / detail | JS pipeline ms (range) | Native pipeline ms (range) | JS / native triangles |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Nine-part coarse | 132.80 (118.62–154.16) | 109.68 (104.48–113.13) | 6,398 / 4,052 |
| Nine-part default | 124.12 (117.37–132.33) | 110.31 (109.01–114.71) | 6,482 / 4,400 |
| Curved coarse | 123.96 (105.12–155.86) | 104.50 (103.14–106.81) | 11,098 / 12,948 |
| Curved default | 97.67 (93.56–112.74) | 187.42 (185.06–188.08) | 16,450 / 23,196 |

The curved default native kernel takes 76.58 ms, but its Python array, normal
and reference extraction takes another 109.75 ms. Comparing that kernel alone
with JS's 96.53 ms meshing stage would reverse the complete pipeline result.
Coarse settings do not guarantee fewer triangles or less CPU with the current
adaptive mesher; these are measured settings, not a monotonicity claim.

| Default payload / whole-process memory | Nine-part JS | Nine-part native | Curved JS | Curved native |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Vertices | 7,523 | 5,532 | 10,306 | 12,596 |
| TESS bytes | 593,696 | 494,272 | 700,396 | 929,068 |
| Gzip bytes | 101,753 | 74,973 | 158,712 | 230,326 |
| Process peak RSS MiB | 229–243 | 302–311 | 218–230 | 276–277 |
| Cached read + JS decode ms | 1.08 | 1.06 | 0.53 | 0.41 |

RSS includes imports, both LODs and audits; it is not incremental mesh memory.
Gzip size is a possible transport cost rather than measured browser latency.
The earlier v1 default JS pipeline was 115.17 ms in its separate study; the
v2 quality fixes do not establish a meshing speedup on that fixture.

## Ownership, validation and remaining limits

Native meshing changes private canonical BREP serialization for all nine
planetary components and four of the seven curved components, even when
serialization omits triangles. Property queries and later extraction preserve
those identities; input files remain byte-for-byte unchanged. A native backend
would therefore need private reconstruction, durable extraction, a separate
mesher identity, and actual renderer/picking/export validation. Candidate
payloads never enter the canonical store or mesh index.

The final shared JS suite passes **967/967 tests**. New regressions exercise
rational circular trims, a thin planar box with short edges, poles/apices,
torus and spline seams, translated profile bounds, analytic normals and frame
handedness. They audit Float32 output for collapsed triangles, orientation,
welded edge incidence and reference ranges, retain existing exact-boundary
tests, and check codec/repeated-byte determinism. Sphere tests also check every
edge midpoint and centroid against its exact radius.

An exploratory **0.01 mm thick curved perforated plate about 105 mm across**
still over-refines into skinny Float32-degenerate triangles. The short-edge
fix is verified on a thin planar box; it does not resolve that curved
high-aspect-ratio case. General thin curved geometry, stronger continuous
error guarantees, and broader STEP visual/picking coverage remain outside
this bounded repair. The FreeCAD retained-document timing study is separate
and must not be inferred from these OCCT-only candidate measurements.
