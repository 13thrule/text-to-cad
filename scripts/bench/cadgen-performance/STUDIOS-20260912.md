# Light and dark studios — 2026-09-12

This records the earlier studio iteration. Its public settings contract is
superseded by [Photographic Render](PHOTOGRAPHIC-RENDER-20260912.md).

## Scope

This follows the CAD/Render separation recorded in
[RENDER-SETTINGS-20260911.md](RENDER-SETTINGS-20260911.md).
Global System/Light/Dark appearance stays in the navbar, using the usual
monitor, sun and moon icons. The last inspector tab starts with a Render
section containing its enable switch, studio and quality controls, Reset,
and collapsed Debug Copy/Paste controls. Copy/Paste remains available with
Render disabled.

There are two studios: `studio-light` and `studio-dark`. An omitted studio
follows global appearance; explicitly choosing either studio pins it for the
model. Reset restores the adaptive choice. The two studios share lighting,
environment and material defaults; their backdrop and floor colors differ.
`render.appearance`, `default` and the former colorful studio IDs are removed.
The snapshot CLI uses the same contract; `--render '{}'` chooses the default
light studio, and `--render studio-dark` chooses the dark backdrop.

The studio environment is generated locally as an HDR softbox room and
prefiltered for physical materials. It does not need the network JPEG used
by the previous default. The viewer and snapshot renderer share the material
and environment code. Changing a studio or quality does not
rebuild exact CAD geometry.

High Render requests a 0.25-pixel screen-error target within the existing
viewport tessellation ladder, L3 geometry for STEP snapshots, 2× snapshot
capture, 4096-pixel shadow maps and a 512-pixel PMREM environment. Opaque model
surfaces receive shadows from themselves and other parts. Alpha-blended parts
do not cast a false opaque silhouette. Normal CAD keeps its simpler shadow
policy. GPU resources are released when replaced, disabled or torn down.
Normal CAD preserves authored color and opacity with matte workbench materials;
Render restores authored physical finishes. The shared environment-intensity
and color-grading paths keep browser and snapshot behavior aligned.

## STEP color correctness

The material fixture exposed a STEPCAF writer issue: differently colored
instances sharing one native TShape inherited the final instance's color.
The exporter now detaches topology only for distinct appearances, sharing the
geometric surfaces and curves. Identical appearances still reuse their export
representative. The saved-document loader keeps whole-part colors on
occurrences rather than baking them into every face of a component.

After regeneration, all 10 material-fixture occurrences have the source colors
and share exactly two canonical geometry components. Explicit per-face colors
also pass the round-trip tests. The corrected fixture rebuilt in 2.41 seconds;
the following ordinary source run reported `current`.

Component schema 20 rekeys newly extracted components. Model-record schema 5
invalidates old generated-source output records so the next source run
regenerates once with the corrected writer. Document mappings stay at schema 4:
saved STEP files remain authoritative, including any colors already lost in
their bytes. The viewer/session settings do not enter the CAD object/index store.

## Review fixtures

Testing uses the warm fixture store under
`models/tmp/quality-refinement-20260911`, with a mounting plate (134 KB STEP),
an impeller (890 KB), a planetary assembly (2.2 MB), and a small authored
10-part material fixture. The latter pairs a sphere and grooved plate in
polished steel, brushed steel, matte blue polymer, black lacquer and copper.
Its initial source build took 2.35 seconds.

Moonwatch is reviewed through its existing cached viewer session. It is not
cold-built or forced through a full assembly snapshot. No generated model,
image or cache is committed for this review.

The impeller's blade facets are authored by a five-segment polygon. Higher
render quality cannot smooth them without changing the source geometry.

## Browser behavior

The small-part browser check verifies the two studio choices, implicit
appearance following, explicit selection of the currently displayed studio,
Reset returning to adaptive behavior, and a custom exposure surviving global
appearance changes, Render disable/reenable and a page reload. Copy/Paste works
with Render disabled; Paste enables it and disabling restores CAD framing.
Reset while disabled stays disabled and preserves the CAD camera. Copied JSON
retains sparse overrides and omits `studio` while adaptive. No browser errors
were reported during those checks.

The final bundled viewer also loaded cached Moonwatch at High detail without
browser errors. Body reference selection worked on the material fixture. The
fine bracelet stripes and concentric dial grooves in Moonwatch are authored
geometry; changing lighting or tessellation does not remove them.

## Measurements and limits

Timing results are complete warm CLI invocations, including PNG output, on
the development machine. They are single-run integration measurements with
other development work running concurrently, not controlled speedup claims.
The baseline is commit `245b16e18` before this studio revision.

| Model | Baseline light | Final light | Baseline dark | Final dark |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Mounting plate | 2.913 s | 1.997 s | 1.888 s | 1.694 s |
| Impeller | 1.928 s | 1.705 s | 2.383 s | 1.680 s |
| Planetary assembly | 2.449 s | 1.743 s | 1.897 s | 1.764 s |
| Material fixture | — | 1.890 s | — | 1.705 s |

All eight final outputs were 1600 × 1200 PNGs from an 800 × 600 request with
High's 2× capture. The material baseline is omitted because its saved STEP
had incorrect occurrence colors. Final outputs use the finer L3 geometry,
HDR softbox environment and 4K shadows. These timings demonstrate that the
quality increase remains practical on these fixtures; the run order and cache
conditions do not support attributing a speedup to this revision.
